# Post-Dungeon — Tharion's Scene (Outline)

**When:** Immediately after the party exits the Ossian Vault  
**Where:** The Iron Tap, Resross (or just outside, if they exit in poor shape)  
**Who:** Tharion Aelindre, Commander Aleth Varne, 1–2 Islefield operatives (unnamed — they hang back)

---

## The Setup

Tharion is waiting. He has been for however many real-world days the party spent inside (remember: 1 hour inside = 1 day outside — they may have been "gone" for several days from Tharion's perspective even if it felt like a few hours to them).

He is composed. He does not show relief. He shows the cloak is there, he nods, and he says:

> *"You're out. Good. Let's sit."*

He buys them food and drink without asking. He orders for them without asking. He reads the room. This is an assessment, and the assessment starts the moment they walk in the door.

---

## What Tharion Lied About (And What The Truth Is)

| What He Said | What Is Actually True |
|---|---|
| "An old Tharnic administrative archive" | An Avethis necromantic dungeon, still fully active |
| "A diplomatic artefact from an Islefield historian — misplaced during the Empire's collapse" | The Cloak of the Watcher's Veil, connection to Balthazar's legacy — he knows more than he said |
| "Should be mostly cleared out by now" | Actively occupied by golems, undead, Weeping Angels, and a potentially active Mummy Lord |
| He said nothing about time | The temporal dilation. He knew the dungeon was "strange with time" — he did not know the 1hr:1day ratio. (This part is true: he underestimated the severity.) |
| He called it a "retrieval job, straightforward in principle" | The locked door requiring Vasskren's cores. The Mummy Lord behind the eye. The Weeping Angels. None of this is straightforward. |

**What he did NOT lie about:**
- The pay (it's real)
- The loot terms (they can keep everything except the cloak)
- His name and affiliation
- The cloak's physical description

---

## The Assessment Criteria

Tharion is watching to see if the party:

1. **Reports the time dilation** — do they know? Do they mention it casually? This tells him they pay attention to magic and consequences.
2. **Asks what the cloak actually does** — not "can we keep it" but genuine curiosity about what they were carrying.
3. **Calls out the undead** — mentions Ossian, the Mummy Lord, the Avethis connection. This tells him how deeply they explored and how much they understand.
4. **Questions the cover story** — presses him on the gap between "old archive" and what they found. This is the one he respects most.
5. **Doesn't push for Balthazar** — he'll note if they come very close to asking the right question and then don't ask it.

**There is no pass/fail.** He is calibrating how much they know, how much they noticed, and how much they can be trusted with partial information.

---

## Reaction Matrix — If They Call Him Out

### If they ask why an "archive" had an active Mummy Lord:
> *"We had intelligence that the dungeon was dormant. The necromancer died sixty years ago. Our source was wrong about the state of the wards. I won't apologise for sending you in with bad information — but I'm not going to pretend the information was good."*

He doesn't apologise. He acknowledges. This is a calculated distinction.

### If they ask about Avethis:
> *"An old god. The dungeon was repurposed by a worshipper. We knew the religious dimension — we chose not to share it because we didn't think it was operationally relevant. We may have been wrong about that."*

He's giving them something. Just not everything.

### If they mention the time dilation specifically:
He goes still. Then: *"How bad?"* He does the math himself, out loud, based on what they tell him. His expression shifts — briefly, genuinely — then it closes again. *"We'll account for that."* He means the days lost. He does not explain why the days matter (the summit timeline, the scroll arriving).

### If they ask what the cloak does:
He will look at it on the table between them and say: *"It belongs to someone. We're returning it."* That is all. DC 18 Insight: this is true, but incomplete — he knows more about the cloak's significance than he's showing. He's not lying here. He just won't explain the significance.

### If they ask who "someone" is:
> *"Someone with standing in Islefield. That's all I can give you."*

DC 16 Persuasion to get more: *"It has been missing for a very long time. Finding it matters to more people than it should, given what it looks like."* He glances at the cloak. That's as far as he'll go.

### If they come very close to naming Balthazar:
He will not react visibly. Internally, this would be significant. He will change the subject to the payment. He will remember whoever said it.

### If they don't call him out at all:
He pays them in full, says *"well done"*, and closes the meeting. Inwardly, he downgrades them slightly. Not enough to matter — they completed the job. But he won't request them by name again without more information.

---

## The Payment Scene

After the conversation runs its natural course, Tharion places the coin on the table. **Agreed rate from the commissioning scene** (see Payments.md). He does not count it in front of them — he simply confirms the amount aloud and slides it across.

If they impressed him (called out at least 2 things from the assessment criteria, specifically asking about the cloak or the time dilation):
- He adds **100 gp** without comment. If asked: *"The job was harder than described. Consider it an adjustment."*
- He will also say, as he stands to leave: *"If you have another commission in the next few months — something that requires people who can navigate that kind of environment without losing their heads — I'll find you."* This is not a vague social nicety. He means it.

---

## What He Never Reveals (Regardless of Skill Checks)

- Balthazar's name
- Why Islefield wants the cloak specifically
- The Second Council's awareness of the party
- The fact that this was partly an assessment
- That he recognises Morjor's name in connection with something beyond the Accord

---

## Closing Beat

Tharion leaves first, with Aleth behind him. He pauses at the door — not to say anything dramatic. He's picking up his coat. Then he says, without turning around:

> *"The dungeon is cleared. Whatever was in there won't bother Resross anymore. You should know that, even if no one else will."*

He leaves.

**DM Note:** This is the only genuine thing he gives them for free. He doesn't have to say it. He says it because the people of Resross near that Heights path — and Vessa Dorn, who knows too much — will be safer now. He cares about this, even though he'll never frame it that way.

---

## If The Party Tries To Follow Them

Aleth will notice immediately (passive Perception 16). He will stop, turn, and wait. He will not say anything. He will simply wait, at a distance, until the party stops following. Tharion will not acknowledge that it happened.

Tharion's group has horses staged outside town. They're moving fast. Without magical travel, the party cannot keep pace.

---

## Loose Threads This Scene Opens

- The party now knows Islefield runs operations like this — covert, funded, with professional operatives
- They know someone in Islefield cares about recovering specific historical items
- If they said anything that suggests they know more than they should (about Balthazar, about the summit's stakes, about the Queen), Tharion will file that information
- If Amdir said anything notable during the conversation, Tharion definitely noted it — the personal history between them gives every exchange a second layer
