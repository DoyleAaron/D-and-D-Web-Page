# Resross — The Prison Town

**Kingdom:** Islefield (administered through Kluimont proxy historically, now Islefield-controlled)  
**Population:** ~4,500 (1,900 permanent settlers/workers/guards, 1,400 prisoners, 1,200 transient/seasonal)  
**Size:** Small town (large for its purpose)  
**Location:** Southeast of Yhey, toward the Ironwarf Heights foothills  
**Notable For:** Penal colony, dragon sightings, unexpected peacefulness, brutal prisoner treatment

---

## Overview

Resross began as a work camp — a place to send the worst offenders from across Islefield and keep them where the mountains kept them from running. The ore from the nearby Heights needed extracting. Prisoners extracted it.

Then families came — wives and husbands of guards, small merchants looking for a captive market, a few enterprising inns, a shrine. And then more families. And then, slowly, a town.

Resross today is genuinely odd. The permanent residents are calm, practical, and not particularly violent. They live in the shadow of something — both literally (the Heights loom over the northwest side of town) and figuratively (the dragon). And the prisoner treatment is brutal by most standards. The two things coexist because the town has sorted itself psychologically: the settlers consider themselves completely separate from the prison. The prison is the prison. The town is the town. They happen to be in the same location.

---

## The Dragon Sightings

Nobody knows which dragon it is. It has been sighted for at least sixty years — always near the upper ridgelines of the Ironwarf Heights, sometimes circling. Never approaching. Never attacking. Scholars from Yhey visited twice and published two separate reports that completely contradicted each other.

What the locals know:
- It is large. Not ancient-large, but significant.
- Its colour is ambiguous — different descriptions say green, grey, or a dark brown.
- It has not harmed anyone in living memory. But a patrol went missing in the Heights thirty years ago, and people are careful about which stories they tell.
- Looking up and seeing a dragon circling your town on a weekly basis has a specific effect on human psychology. People stop worrying about small things. Disputes get resolved faster. Arguments feel less important. *The dragon is still out there.* Everything else is minor.

**DM Note on the dragon:** Up to you. It could be Bagi (the brass wyrm from the world lore), a reclusive dragon with no plot relevance, or the beginning of something for a future session. Residents won't talk about it as a threat — it's almost a mascot. But they don't name it.

## The Prison

**Resross Hold** — a walled compound on the eastern side of town. Two layers of wall, guard towers. The interior is not visible from the town proper.

- The prisoners are **the worst** — murderers, traitors to the crown, those convicted of high magic crimes. The ones who are not executed are sent here.
- Treatment is harsh. Lashings are common. Deaths from punishment are not uncommon and are officially recorded as "complications of sentence."
- The guards are not monsters — most are just doing a job. But the job normalises things that shouldn't be normal.
- **Prisoner resentment is extremely high.** Escaped prisoners are the primary source of violent crime in the surrounding region, which is genuinely rare but spikes twice a year.

**What the party notices (if they pay attention):**
- The prison side of town is downwind. The smell is distinct.
- Prisoner work gangs are sometimes visible on the road — chained, moving ore carts from the Heights.
- Locals don't look at the work gangs. Not out of cruelty. Out of habit. It is a practiced non-seeing.

## Town Personality

**Not hostile to strangers.** Resross gets enough traffic from guard rotations and supply wagons that outsiders are not unusual. The locals are straightforward — they don't gossip the way port towns do, and they don't have the elven social strata of Yhey. Everyone who lives here chose to. That creates a certain grim confidence.

**The shrine** near the town centre is dedicated to no specific god — multiple small icons, people from many backgrounds. The shrine keeper is a woman named **Vessa Dorn**, late fifties, sharp eyes. She has lived here forty years. She knows everything about everyone. She is the closest thing to an information source the town has.

---

## Key Locations

- **The Iron Tap** — The main inn. Functional, clean, inexpensive. The common room is quiet for a tavern. Nobody drinks to be loud here.
- **Warden's Office** — Where prisoners and supply chain logistics are managed. The current Warden, **Halden Cosse**, is a bureaucrat who inherited the position. He does not enjoy it but is not cruel.
- **The Foundry Gate** — Entry point to the Heights ore route. Guarded. The party would need papers from the Warden or a strong bluff (DC 15) to use this route.
- **The shrine (Vessa Dorn)** — Small, round-walled building. Always has a lit candle visible from outside.

## Relevance to the Dungeon

The Ossian Vault is approximately **6 miles northeast of Resross** along a narrow path that veers off the main Heights road. No sign. No marker. Most locals don't know it exists — there are many old Tharnic structures in the Heights, and only a few have been catalogued.

**Vessa Dorn** knows something: *"There is an old ruin up the second fork of the upper road. Not the mine — the other one. Nobody goes there. A Warden's patrol went to investigate about fifteen years ago and came back two days later thinking they'd been gone an hour."* (This is the time dilation effect.)

**Accessing the dungeon from Resross:**
- 2-hour walk on foot through rough terrain
- The hidden side entrance (from the cellar) exits to a narrow ravine about 200 ft from the main road
- Players who find the side entrance before the front entrance have a tactical advantage (enter the left wing directly)
- **Pipe system:** A network of old Tharnic drainage pipes exits through the cliff face approximately 80 ft below and east of the main entrance — rusted iron mouths in the rock, partially overgrown. DC 14 Investigation (searching the cliff) or DC 16 passive Perception to spot. Medium creatures can crawl through (tight quarters). This bypasses the main entrance entirely and leads into the cellar (Room F). Time dilation begins the moment a creature enters the pipe from outside.
- **DM note — Liora's door:** Inside the pipe junction (see Dungeon Overview Q) there is a hidden door only Morjor can open. Its position is noted; content is not yet prepped.
