# Room 17 — Notes & Documents

## Ossian's Research Codex (stone alcove — no check needed, wrapped in oilskin)
Ossian's complete private manuscript — illuminated, Common and Old Elvish. His life's work. DC 12 History to read the Common sections; Old Elvish sections require a Language check or *Comprehend Languages*.

---

> *"Entry 1 — What this place is.*
>
> *I was wrong about why I came here. I came because the surveys mentioned unusual stone formations in the lower tunnels and a thaumic resonance no one could explain. I thought I was investigating a natural occurrence. I was not. I was following directions. Someone had arranged for a person like me to find this, in the same way you arrange furniture for a guest you expect: deliberately, in advance, with specific intent.*
>
> *Note: I am not troubled by this. I am interested in it."*

> *"Entry 4 — What was here first.*
>
> *The inscription on the dais in the Grand Hall predates this facility by centuries. I had it dated. The stone is non-local \u2014 brought here, shaped, placed, inscribed by one set of hands. The inscription reads: 'a witness was needed.'*
>
> *You do not say 'a witness was needed' unless you are about to do something that matters. Something you want recorded.*
>
> *One being. Alone. Building a place for something to watch from. I have thought about this for a very long time."*

> *"Entry 7 — The maker. The old texts give him a name: Gaia.*
>
> *I write it plainly because the name is ancient and, I am almost certain, safe. It sleeps in a hundred mouldering manuscripts and means nothing to anyone living. Gaia is not a god in the conventional sense. He does not receive worship or maintain interest in worshippers. He is the earth. The literal earth — this ground, the rock under it, the heat at the centre, the roots that hold the whole thing together. That is him, or he is an expression of that, or the distinction between 'him' and 'it' does not survive contact with what he actually is.*
>
> *He came here \u2014 made himself small enough to come here, which is the part of this I find genuinely difficult to comprehend \u2014 because he wanted to make something."*

> *"Entry 9 — What he made.*
>
> *Three records. He created three things in this room that are records of three acts. Not tools. Not weapons. Records. The way a handprint in clay is a record of a hand.*
>
> *The first: he gave form to something formless. The second: he gave persistence to something temporary. The third: he gave boundary to something unbounded.*
>
> *These are not metaphors. These are descriptions of things that happened.*
>
> *The records he made of them: one is a book — the present age calls it the Chart of the Threefold Way. One is a ring. One is a crown. They were made here. They are no longer here."*

> *"Entry 11 — The Eye.*
>
> *He did not leave the records here for safekeeping. He left them here because this is where they were made, and you do not move the record of something from the place where it happened. That is not how records work.*
>
> *What he chose to leave here, deliberately, was the Eye.*
>
> *The Eye is not a structure. It is a being. He made it here, in this room, as a companion \u2014 something to watch while he worked. Something that would see what he did and remember it, because he needed a witness, because he was alone in the way a first thing is alone: completely, without category.*
>
> *He made a friend out of witnessing, because witnessing was the only thing he had to give.*
>
> *My followers call it Avethis. They believe they are worshipping the Eye of some ancient pantheon. They are not. They are worshipping a thing made out of loneliness — a companion the soul of the world built so it would not have to make everything alone, and then outlived. A minor god of pure witness, present in every room of every life ever lived. I find that unbearably sad. I have not told them. I do not know that it would change anything.*
>
> *The Eye could not follow when he left. It can only watch. It has been watching since before this stone was cut, before this hill existed, before any record that any scholar has ever consulted.*
>
> *Note: Gaia is approximately as powerful as the world, because he is the world. The Eye he made carries a continuity of witness older than the gods. It did not ask to be what it is. It has no interest in worship. It watches because that is its entire nature. And because he made it, and he is what he is, the Eye is — functionally, for anyone who encounters it — a power that does not have a smaller word than deity.*
>
> *I have been in this room for four days. I have not been harmed. I have been seen. Completely. Without remainder. These are different things."*

> *"Entry 14 — The part I swore I would not write.*
>
> *Gaia did not die. I need to be precise, because everything turns on the precision: Gaia did not die, did not leave in the way a person leaves, did not end. The earth does not end. He is still here. Still moving the way a world moves. Still — and this is what took me eleven months to accept — still acting. Still making arrangements. Still, apparently, capable of wanting things.*
>
> *And he wears a name now. A present name. One the living would recognise, because the living have met him, dealt with him, taken his instructions without once suspecting what they were standing in front of.*
>
> *I said I would never write it. I am writing it exactly once, here, in the one room where it belongs, because whoever reaches this far has earned the truth and will need it:*
>
> *Gaia is the one they call Balthazar.*
>
> *Balthazar is not a man who became powerful. Balthazar is what the soul of the world is calling itself in this age — the current shape of a thing older than shape. Everything laid at the archmage's feet — the vault, the Three, the chosen messengers, the long patient designs — is not the work of a wizard. It is the work of the ground you are standing on, wearing a name and a face the way you or I would wear a coat.*
>
> *I have written this nowhere else, and I will not. If you are holding the Codex, you are holding the only place the two names sit on the same page."*

> *"Final entry.*
>
> *I am going to stay here.*
>
> *Not because I have to. Because this room contains the most significant fact I have ever learned, and leaving it unguarded is not something I am willing to do. I have made it as hard as I can to reach this room carelessly. If you are reading this: you were not careless. You were thorough, and you fought for what Vasskren carried, and you placed the pieces correctly.*
>
> *The Eye has already seen you. It sees everything. Welcome to the record."*

## Slip of Paper (tucked between the last pages — Perception DC 16 to notice it is a separate insert)
Ossian's hand, smaller and more cramped than the rest of the Codex \u2014 written last, almost as an afterthought:

> *"Transfer document. Colony Year 31. Three items removed from the lower eastern archive, recorded as 'survey materials, misc.' Recipient: Islefield Administrative Authority. Storage: lower archive, administrative quarter, sealed section. Signed by a name I recognise from old Second Council records. This was not routine. This was filed by someone who knew the items were not ordinary survey materials, who wanted them somewhere specific.*
>
> *The records of Gaia are in Islefield. Under it, to be precise: beneath the old administrative quarter, in a sealed lower archive. I do not know if anyone there knows what they are."*

*(Easy to miss \u2014 the slip is slightly smaller than the Codex pages and has no heading.)*
