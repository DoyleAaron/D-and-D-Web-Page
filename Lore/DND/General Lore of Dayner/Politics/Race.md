# Peoples and Social Relations

Dayner's ancestry, citizenship, class, religion, and place of birth all influence how a person is treated. Kingdom-wide summaries are useful, but local custom often matters more than national law.

## Braewood

[[Kingdoms/Braewood/Braewood|Braewood]] is human-majority and legally permits many peoples to settle, trade, and serve. Treatment varies sharply by region.

- Humans dominate the Crown, army, noble offices, and central administration.
- Dwarven communities possess significant wealth and autonomy in the north but remain wary of royal control after centuries of exploitation.
- Orcs, goblins, half-orcs, tieflings, and visibly unusual peoples may face harassment, exclusion, or violence in hostile settlements.
- Large trade centres are usually more practical than isolated communities, though status and sponsorship still matter.
- Memories of the human-led Tharnic Empire shape distrust in places such as Borugham and Yotherdon.

## Islefield

[[Kingdoms/Islefield/Islefield|Islefield]] is almost evenly divided between elves and humans, but political and cultural power remains strongly elven.

- Ancient elven houses control the monarchy, sacred institutions, extensive land, and many senior offices.
- Humans can become wealthy merchants, officers, and professionals but rarely enter the oldest circles of power.
- Half-elves often occupy an unstable middle position: accepted in mixed districts but reminded of their ancestry in elite settings.
- [[Kingdoms/Islefield/Medium Cities/Cheling|Cheling]] uses social preference and contempt more often than legal exclusion.
- [[Kingdoms/Islefield/Medium Cities/Yhey|Yhey]] is more openly abusive, particularly toward Kluimont residents, orcs, goblins, and poor humans.
- [[Kingdoms/Islefield/Village/Sable|Sable]] and [[Kingdoms/Islefield/POI/Shercam Woods|Shercam Woods]] permit only elves beyond their outer boundary. Elves born outside recognised Shercam traditions may also be shunned.

## Kluimont

[[Kingdoms/Kluimont/Kluimont|Kluimont]] has Dayner's highest proportion of orc and goblin residents and is generally more accepting of peoples considered monstrous elsewhere.

- Orcs, goblins, humans, tieflings, dragonborn, and mixed families are common across mainland settlements and the Rimduff Isles.
- Acceptance is practical rather than universal. Poverty, dangerous work, weak mainland services, and arcane elitism still create inequality.
- The capital island judges magical credentials, sponsorship, and security status as strongly as ancestry.
- Border communities such as [[Kingdoms/Kluimont/Village/Ukrolis|Ukrolis]] and [[Kingdoms/Kluimont/Village/Ansdale|Ansdale]] develop more mixed customs through constant contact.
- Erymont's violent warbands should not be treated as representative of Kluimont's ordinary orc and goblin communities.

## Lavalto

[[Kingdoms/Lavalto/Lavalto|Lavalto]] is publicly diverse but governed through family power, secrecy, and coercion.

- Darwaeton's port includes humans, orcs, goblins, dwarves, tieflings, elves, halflings, gnomes, and travellers from every kingdom.
- Flison is hostile toward mainland humans and elves because of its imperial military culture.
- Agosron's social order separates pureblood households from everyone beneath them more sharply than ordinary ancestry categories suggest.
- Vampires conceal themselves within noble, civic, religious, and merchant structures. Their legal status is less important than their control of debts and dependants.

## General Patterns

- **Trade softens barriers:** Ports, crossroads, and mixed border towns usually value useful skills and reliable coin.
- **Institutions preserve barriers:** Noble houses, sacred orders, military offices, and arcane councils can exclude people without changing written law.
- **Local history matters:** A settlement shaped by raids, enslavement, or forced relocation may distrust a group welcomed elsewhere in the same kingdom.
- **Wealth and sponsorship protect:** Foreigners with royal papers, guild credentials, or powerful patrons receive treatment denied to poorer people of the same ancestry.
